# Case Study: Supply shortage in a ride marketplace

**Category:** Marketplace / Mobility  
**Suggested time:** 30-35 minutes  
**Difficulty:** Intermediate to Advanced  
**Scenario status:** Fictional practice case. All organisations, figures, and situations are invented for learning.

## Candidate brief

A fictional ride marketplace has enough passenger demand in a city but experiences long pickup times and cancelled rides in several outer zones. The company is considering higher driver incentives.

## Information supplied

Fictional data: core zones have 82% request fulfilment and average pickup time of 6 minutes. Outer zones have 57% fulfilment and 14-minute pickup time. Driver online hours in outer zones are 30% lower than forecast. Passenger demand spikes around two transit hubs and late evenings. Incentive spend is already rising.

## Your assignment

Recommend how to improve marketplace availability without creating uncontrolled incentive costs.

## Work area

Before reading the facilitator notes, write your response using these headings:

1. Objective and assumptions.
2. User / stakeholder segments.
3. Problem statement.
4. Evidence to inspect and questions to ask.
5. Options considered.
6. Recommendation and first release.
7. Metrics and guardrails.

## Candidate prompts and hints

Segment by zone and time. Investigate driver earnings, dead kilometres, trip destination, safety, wait time, demand predictability, and cancellation reasons. Ask whether the problem is absolute supply, supply positioning, or matching.

## Facilitator notes: one defensible approach

Pilot time- and zone-specific supply interventions, such as clearer demand forecasts, repositioning guidance, targeted guaranteed earnings, or pickup-area changes. Pair incentives with supply retention and service-quality measures. Avoid blanket bonuses before understanding driver economics.

## Suggested success measures

Primary: fulfilled requests in target zone-time cells. Guardrails: driver net earnings per online hour, passenger wait time, cancellation rate, incentive cost per incremental trip, safety incidents, and driver retention.

## Follow-up probes

How do you avoid shifting the shortage to another zone? What if more incentives attract low-quality supply? How do you estimate incremental trips caused by an incentive?
