# Senior / Lead / Group PM Preparation Path

Senior interviews usually place more emphasis on ambiguity, strategy, organisational influence, and outcomes across teams. The exact expectations depend on company size, product maturity, and role scope.

## 1. Build a portfolio of decisions

Prepare 4-6 examples that demonstrate:

- Selecting a problem worth solving.
- Setting strategy for a product area.
- Making a material investment or prioritisation trade-off.
- Influencing teams or executives without direct authority.
- Improving a product or business metric.
- Stopping or changing a direction when evidence changed.

For each example, explain scope, your role, decision rights, stakeholders, options, result, and learning.

## 2. Practise strategy at multiple levels

Move beyond a feature roadmap. Practise:

- Product-area strategy and positioning.
- Portfolio and resource allocation.
- Build, buy, partner, and platform choices.
- Market entry and segment focus.
- Balancing short-term delivery with long-term capability.
- Creating operating mechanisms for discovery and outcomes.

## 3. Show leverage through others

Prepare examples of coaching PMs, improving decision quality, clarifying team interfaces, and creating mechanisms that make teams more effective. Leadership is not measured only by headcount or seniority.

## 4. Prepare for executive communication

Practise a two-minute recommendation that includes:

- Decision required.
- Customer and business context.
- Options and trade-offs.
- Recommendation.
- Investment and risk.
- Milestones and outcome measures.

## 5. Handle ambiguity explicitly

Senior prompts may have no clear right answer. Show how you define the decision, identify reversible versus irreversible choices, set evidence thresholds, and make a staged commitment.

## 6. Prepare for hard follow-ups

- What did you stop doing?
- What was the opportunity cost?
- What did you get wrong?
- How did you align people who disagreed?
- What would happen if the budget were cut?
- How did the organisation become better because of your work?

## 7. Avoid senior-level traps

- Speaking only in vision language without operational detail.
- Taking credit for outcomes without explaining personal contribution.
- Using team size as a substitute for impact.
- Treating consensus as the only successful outcome.
- Avoiding hard decisions or lessons from failure.
- Giving a roadmap without resource and capability choices.

## Senior readiness exercise

Prepare a 10-minute review of a product area you have owned or studied. Include the current state, strategic choices, investment trade-offs, operating model, outcome measures, and one decision you would make differently.
