# Practice Exercise: Enter a New Market

## Scenario

A fictional Indian SaaS company sells invoicing software to small businesses in three metro areas. Leadership is considering expansion into smaller cities. The product has a stable core invoicing workflow, but the company has limited local sales and support capacity.

All details are fictional and provided for practice.

## Your assignment

Prepare a 5-minute recommendation: enter now, run a limited pilot, or defer. Make your decision for the next two quarters.

## Information supplied

- Current customers: 12,000 small businesses.
- Current monthly customer churn: 2.5%.
- Support tickets are concentrated around tax setup, integrations, and onboarding.
- The sales team can support one new region at a time.
- Product engineering has capacity for one major initiative this quarter.
- No reliable market-size estimate is available.

## Work through the decision

1. **Clarify the objective:** Is leadership seeking revenue growth, customer reach, lower acquisition cost, or strategic positioning? State a working assumption if not supplied.
2. **Segment:** Identify which smaller-city business types have a sufficiently similar workflow and a reachable buying channel.
3. **Validate demand:** Specify interviews, sales discovery, partner conversations, and paid-pilot evidence.
4. **Assess readiness:** Review onboarding, tax configuration, support coverage, integrations, and payment or data requirements.
5. **Compare options:** Full rollout, one-city pilot, partner-led pilot, or defer while fixing retention and onboarding.
6. **Recommend:** Choose one path and explain the opportunity cost.
7. **Define gates:** Set explicit conditions for continuing, expanding, or stopping.

## Example recommendation direction

A limited, partner-supported pilot is defensible if discovery shows a reachable segment with strong demand and a partner can provide local onboarding. Do not assume this is automatically the best answer. A candidate could recommend deferral if churn, support burden, or product readiness indicates that expansion would amplify existing weaknesses.

## Example pilot scorecard

| Area | Measure | Decision use |
|---|---|---|
| Demand | Qualified pipeline and paid conversion | Is there genuine willingness to buy? |
| Activation | Time to first invoice and first repeat workflow | Can customers reach value? |
| Retention | Cohort retention after 30/60 days | Does value persist? |
| Economics | Acquisition cost and contribution margin | Can the motion scale? |
| Operations | Tickets per active customer and resolution time | Can the team support the market? |

## Follow-up probes

- What evidence would make you stop the pilot?
- What would you do if demand is strong but onboarding completion is weak?
- How would your answer change if a competitor entered the region?
- Which capability would you build first if the pilot succeeds?

## Self-review

A strong answer makes a choice, shows the assumptions behind it, includes an alternative, respects capacity constraints, and sets measurable decision gates.
