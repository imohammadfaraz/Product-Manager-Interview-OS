# Company Research Playbook

## Research with a decision in mind

Your aim is not to memorise a company profile. It is to understand why the role exists, which customer and business problems matter, and how your experience could help.

## Step 1: Understand the company

Use current public sources to identify:

- The primary customer segments.
- The job the product helps customers complete.
- Core products and how they fit together.
- Revenue model and major cost drivers, where public information exists.
- Main alternatives and competitive pressures.
- Recent strategic priorities, launches, or market changes.

Prefer official product pages, public filings, investor material, engineering blogs, release notes, and credible reporting. Record publication dates and distinguish company statements from third-party interpretation.

## Step 2: Understand the role

Read the job description line by line. For every responsibility, write:

- What outcome might this role own?
- Which users or internal stakeholders are involved?
- Which decisions are likely in scope?
- Which competencies are needed?
- What evidence from my experience demonstrates the competency?

Do not assume the job description tells the whole story. Ask the recruiter or interviewer about scope when possible.

## Step 3: Analyse the product

Choose one important journey. Use the product if possible. Observe onboarding, activation, core workflow, recovery, pricing, support, and cross-device experience where relevant. If you cannot access it, use public documentation and state the limitation.

## Step 4: Form a point of view

Use this structure:

- **Observation:** What can be seen in the product or public evidence?
- **Interpretation:** What might this mean for a customer or the business?
- **Hypothesis:** What could be improved and why?
- **Validation:** What evidence would confirm or disprove it?
- **Trade-off:** What might get worse if your idea is implemented?

Avoid saying "the company should" based on a quick personal impression. Treat your teardown as a hypothesis, not a verdict.

## Step 5: Prepare your experience evidence

Map three to five relevant stories to the role's needs. Show scope, decisions, outcomes, and lessons. Use only accurate metrics and explain their definitions and timeframe.

## Step 6: Prepare interviewer questions

Good questions explore strategy, team decision-making, customer learning, product constraints, success measures, and role expectations. Avoid asking for information easily found on the company website.

## 45-minute research timebox

- 10 min: company and business model.
- 10 min: role and job description.
- 15 min: product journey and one teardown hypothesis.
- 5 min: map your evidence.
- 5 min: write interviewer questions.

Save your sources and date checked. Refresh them before the interview.
