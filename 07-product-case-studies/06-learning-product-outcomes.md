# Case Study: Improve learning outcomes in a mobile learning app

**Category:** EdTech / Learning  
**Suggested time:** 30-35 minutes  
**Difficulty:** Intermediate to Advanced  
**Scenario status:** Fictional practice case. All organisations, figures, and situations are invented for learning.

## Candidate brief

A fictional learning app has high lesson starts but low course completion. Leadership wants to increase engagement. The PM suspects the product is optimising for activity rather than learning.

## Information supplied

Fictional data: 100,000 learners start a course. 65% complete lesson one, 38% reach lesson five, and 12% complete the course. Learners who attempt weekly practice are more likely to pass a final skill assessment, but only 20% attempt practice.

## Your assignment

Recommend a product strategy that improves demonstrated learning, not just time spent in the app.

## Work area

Before reading the facilitator notes, write your response using these headings:

1. Objective and assumptions.
2. User / stakeholder segments.
3. Problem statement.
4. Evidence to inspect and questions to ask.
5. Options considered.
6. Recommendation and first release.
7. Metrics and guardrails.
8. Risks, dependencies, and decision gates.

## Candidate prompts and hints

Ask what skill the course teaches, how assessment validity is established, and whether users have time or device constraints. Segment by learner goal, baseline skill, course length, and available time.

## Facilitator notes: one defensible approach

Make a measurable learning milestone visible early. Test shorter practice loops, spaced review, progress feedback, and schedule-aware reminders. Validate actual skill through a task or assessment, not only lesson completion.

## Suggested success measures

Primary: share of learners demonstrating the target skill after a defined period. Leading: practice completion, retrieval success, lesson-to-practice transition. Guardrails: learner stress, accessibility, dropout, and assessment fairness.

## Follow-up probes

What if users want a certificate rather than mastery? How do you avoid reminders becoming spam? What if the assessment is a poor proxy for real skill?
