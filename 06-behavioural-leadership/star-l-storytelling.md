# STAR-L: Tell Behavioural Stories with Evidence

STAR-L stands for Situation, Task, Action, Result, and Learning. Use it as a memory aid, not a rigid script.

## S: Situation

Give enough context for the interviewer to understand the stakes. Include the product, customer, team, or business context. Keep background short.

## T: Task

State the goal and your responsibility. What were you accountable for? What was difficult or uncertain? Distinguish your responsibility from the team's overall responsibility.

## A: Action

Spend the most time here. Explain:

- What you personally did.
- What evidence you gathered.
- Which options you considered.
- How you made the decision.
- How you involved other people.
- What you changed when something did not work.

Use "I" for your contribution and "we" when describing shared work. Do not take credit for someone else's work.

## R: Result

State the outcome with context. Use numbers when you have reliable numbers. If the result was qualitative, describe the observable change. Explain attribution carefully: the team's result may have several causes.

## L: Learning

Say what you learned and what you would do differently next time. Show that the lesson affected your later behaviour.

## A concise example structure

"At [context], we faced [problem and stakes]. I was responsible for [scope]. I first [diagnosis], then considered [options]. I chose [decision] because [reason], aligned [partners], and managed [trade-off]. The result was [evidence]. I learned [lesson], which I later applied by [subsequent behaviour]."

## Make the story credible

- Include a real constraint or disagreement.
- Explain the decision, not just the activity.
- Be specific about your role.
- Use numbers only when you can explain their source and timeframe.
- Do not manufacture a failure to make the story sound dramatic.
- Be ready to answer what you would change.

## Build coverage

Aim for stories covering:

1. A product decision with measurable impact.
2. A difficult stakeholder disagreement.
3. A failure or missed outcome.
4. A decision made with incomplete data.
5. A prioritisation trade-off.
6. A cross-functional delivery challenge.
7. A customer insight that changed your plan.
8. A time you influenced without authority.
9. A time you simplified or stopped work.
10. A time you helped another person or team improve.

A single story can cover multiple competencies, but do not force it to answer every question.
