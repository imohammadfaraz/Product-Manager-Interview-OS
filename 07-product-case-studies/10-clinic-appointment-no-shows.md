# Case Study: Reduce missed clinic appointments

**Category:** Healthcare / Service Operations  
**Suggested time:** 30-35 minutes  
**Difficulty:** Intermediate to Advanced  
**Scenario status:** Fictional practice case. All organisations, figures, and situations are invented for learning.

## Candidate brief

A fictional clinic network has high patient no-shows. Operations asks the product team to add more reminders. The PM must determine whether reminders are the right intervention.

## Information supplied

Fictional monthly data: 8,000 appointments booked; 14% are no-shows; 9% are cancelled with less than 12 hours notice. No-show rates are higher for appointments booked more than 21 days in advance. Some patients report transport and work-schedule uncertainty.

## Your assignment

Diagnose the problem and propose a focused intervention that improves attendance without making access harder.

## Work area

Before reading the facilitator notes, write your response using these headings:

1. Objective and assumptions.
2. User / stakeholder segments.
3. Problem statement.
4. Evidence to inspect and questions to ask.
5. Options considered.
6. Recommendation and first release.
7. Metrics and guardrails.
8. Risks, dependencies, and decision gates.

## Candidate prompts and hints

Separate patient intent, appointment lead time, reminder delivery, clinic wait expectations, booking channel, transport, and cancellation friction. Consider whether patients can easily reschedule or cancel.

## Facilitator notes: one defensible approach

Pilot two-way confirmation and low-friction rescheduling for appointments with longer lead times. Pair reminders with accurate preparation and location information. Consider an appropriate waitlist process. Avoid punitive fees without understanding access and equity implications.

## Suggested success measures

Primary: attended appointments divided by appointments due. Guardrails: cancellation friction, time to next available appointment, patient complaints, clinician idle time, and access for urgent patients.

## Follow-up probes

What if reminders increase cancellations but reduce no-shows? How do you avoid disadvantaging patients with limited phone access?
