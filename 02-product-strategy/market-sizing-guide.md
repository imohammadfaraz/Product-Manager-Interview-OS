# Market Sizing Guide for PM Interviews

## What market sizing is for

Market sizing helps you reason about the scale of an opportunity and the assumptions that drive it. Interviewers usually care more about a transparent, defensible model than a precise-looking answer.

## Choose the right method

- **Top-down:** Start with a broad market estimate and narrow it using relevant segments. Useful when credible market-level data is supplied.
- **Bottom-up:** Estimate reachable customers and their usage or spend. Often easier to explain when you can make clear behavioural assumptions.
- **Value-based:** Estimate the economic value created or spend displaced. Useful for B2B or workflow products when value can be modelled.

## Bottom-up example

Prompt: Estimate the annual revenue opportunity for a hypothetical appointment-management SaaS product serving independent clinics in one country.

State assumptions rather than presenting them as facts:

1. Addressable clinics: assume 100,000 for the exercise.
2. Share with a relevant operational need and ability to adopt: assume 40%.
3. Reachable share in the first planning period: assume 10% of that segment.
4. Annual subscription: assume ₹24,000 per clinic.

Calculation:

- Relevant clinics = 100,000 × 40% = 40,000.
- Reachable clinics = 40,000 × 10% = 4,000.
- Annual revenue opportunity at full adoption of the reachable group = 4,000 × ₹24,000 = ₹96,000,000, or ₹9.6 crore.

This is an illustrative model only. A real decision would need verified clinic counts, segmentation, willingness-to-pay evidence, acquisition capacity, churn, support costs, and competition. The estimate is not a forecast.

## How to communicate the estimate

Say: "Using these assumptions, I estimate roughly ₹9.6 crore of annual subscription revenue within the initially reachable segment. The two most sensitive assumptions are the number of relevant clinics and the adoption rate. I would validate those before treating this as an investment case."

## Sensitivity analysis

Change one important assumption at a time. For example, test adoption at 5%, 10%, and 15%. A range reveals whether the decision depends on an uncertain input.

## Common mistakes

- Mixing users, accounts, transactions, and revenue.
- Multiplying by a percentage without defining its denominator.
- Using the entire population as the addressable market.
- Forgetting frequency or annualisation.
- Treating assumed inputs as researched facts.
- Ignoring the ability to reach, serve, and retain customers.

## Candidate checklist

- [ ] I defined the market and geography.
- [ ] I selected top-down, bottom-up, or value-based reasoning and explained why.
- [ ] I stated every major assumption.
- [ ] My units are consistent.
- [ ] I showed the formula and arithmetic.
- [ ] I identified the inputs with the greatest uncertainty.
- [ ] I explained what I would validate next.
