# Case Study: Improve trust in a home-services marketplace

**Category:** Marketplace  
**Suggested time:** 30-35 minutes  
**Difficulty:** Intermediate to Advanced  
**Scenario status:** Fictional practice case. All organisations, figures, and situations are invented for learning.

## Candidate brief

A fictional marketplace connects customers with independent home-service providers. Bookings are growing, but customer complaints about inconsistent quality and disputed charges are increasing.

## Information supplied

Fictional quarterly data: bookings increased 25%. Repeat booking rate is 31%. Customer complaints increased 40%. The top complaint categories are late arrival, final price higher than estimate, and incomplete work. Provider acceptance is 78%, and cancellations after acceptance are 9%.

## Your assignment

Recommend how to improve customer trust while preserving provider participation and marketplace liquidity.

## Work area

Before reading the facilitator notes, write your response using these headings:

1. Objective and assumptions.
2. User / stakeholder segments.
3. Problem statement.
4. Evidence to inspect and questions to ask.
5. Options considered.
6. Recommendation and first release.
7. Metrics and guardrails.
8. Risks, dependencies, and decision gates.

## Candidate prompts and hints

Map discovery, quote, booking, arrival, service, payment, and dispute. Interview both sides. Examine whether estimates are binding, what creates price changes, and how quality is verified.

## Facilitator notes: one defensible approach

Start with transparent scope and price-change approval, clear arrival windows, and a simple service-completion confirmation. Pilot quality checks for selected categories. Avoid a heavy rating penalty until evidence distinguishes provider misconduct from poor job scoping or customer expectation gaps.

## Suggested success measures

Primary: bookings completed without a material dispute. Guardrails: provider retention, cancellation, price variance, repeat booking, time to resolve disputes, and unfair penalty rate.

## Follow-up probes

What if providers reject fixed prices? How do you distinguish an honest scope change from overcharging? What would you do about repeat poor-quality providers?
