# Case Study: Grocery delivery retention

**Category:** Consumer / Marketplace  
**Suggested time:** 30-35 minutes  
**Difficulty:** Intermediate to Advanced  
**Scenario status:** Fictional practice case. All organisations, figures, and situations are invented for learning.

## Candidate brief

A fictional grocery delivery app has grown first orders through discounts, but fewer customers place a second order. Leadership asks for a retention plan that does not rely on increasing discounts.

## Information supplied

Fictional four-week cohort: 20,000 first-time customers. 42% place a second order within 30 days. Repeat rate is 51% for orders delivered within the promised window and 28% for orders delivered late. 18% of first orders contain at least one unavailable item. Discounted first orders have a lower second-order rate than full-price first orders, but customer mix differs by channel.

## Your assignment

Diagnose likely retention drivers and recommend a focused 60-day plan. Avoid claiming correlation proves causation.

## Work area

Before reading the facilitator notes, write your response using these headings:

1. Objective and assumptions.
2. User / stakeholder segments.
3. Problem statement.
4. Evidence to inspect and questions to ask.
5. Options considered.
6. Recommendation and first release.
7. Metrics and guardrails.
8. Risks, dependencies, and decision gates.

## Candidate prompts and hints

Explore delivery reliability, item availability, substitutions, basket fit, price perception, and acquisition intent. Segment by city, zone, basket type, first-order promotion, and fulfilment quality. Ask whether the data controls for customer and channel mix.

## Facilitator notes: one defensible approach

Treat reliability and item availability as high-priority hypotheses because the supplied data suggests a meaningful association. Validate with cohort cuts and customer research. Pilot operational and product changes in a small zone: clearer substitution preferences, availability confidence, and proactive issue resolution. Do not simply remove discounts for everyone.

## Suggested success measures

Primary: 30-day second-order rate by comparable cohort. Leading: on-time delivery, item fill rate, successful substitution, first-order issue resolution. Guardrails: contribution margin, refunds, complaints, and picker/delivery partner workload.

## Follow-up probes

What if late delivery is caused by weather? How do you separate discount impact from customer intent? What would you test in one zone?
