# Aspiring Product Manager Preparation Path

This path is for people who have not held a formal Product Manager title. You can demonstrate PM skills through adjacent work, education, internships, volunteering, and self-directed projects. Be transparent about the context and your level of ownership.

## Step 1: Learn the actual job

Read the [Interview Handbook](../00-getting-started/pm-interview-handbook.md). Write a plain-language explanation of what a PM does across discovery, strategy, delivery, measurement, and collaboration.

## Step 2: Create evidence through a small project

Choose a product problem you can investigate ethically and realistically. Examples:

- Improve an existing public-service or community workflow.
- Study a student or early-career job-search problem.
- Analyse onboarding for a product you can use.
- Prototype a simple solution to an observed task.

Do not pretend a self-directed project had real users or business impact if it did not. Separate observed evidence, interviews, prototype feedback, and assumptions.

## Step 3: Produce a PM portfolio artefact

Create a short case with:

1. Problem and target user.
2. Research method and limitations.
3. Insights and opportunity selection.
4. Proposed solution and alternatives.
5. Prototype or workflow.
6. Success measures and risks.
7. What you learned and what you would do next.

## Step 4: Practise the core rounds

- Product Sense: answer 10 prompts.
- Strategy: practise market sizing and trade-offs.
- Metrics: build a metric tree and diagnose changes.
- Execution: explain how you would work with engineering and design.
- Behavioural: use examples from projects, study, or volunteering.

## Step 5: Build honest behavioural stories

Stories can come from non-PM settings if the competencies are relevant. Explain the context clearly. Do not rename a student project as professional product ownership.

## Step 6: Prepare for entry-level scope

Show curiosity, structure, learning speed, communication, reliability, and an ability to work through a scoped problem. Do not try to sound like a Group PM. Demonstrate readiness for the responsibilities of the role you are applying for.

## Suggested weekly output

- One product analysis.
- One interview case.
- One story rehearsal.
- One feedback conversation.
- One improvement to your portfolio artefact.
