# Execution & Prioritisation Question Bank

### 1. Engineering says a committed feature will take six months instead of two. What do you do?
- **Level / time:** Intermediate / 25 min.
- **Tests:** Discovery of constraints, scope, trade-offs, communication.
- **Task:** Produce a response plan and recommendation.
- **Strong signals:** Understands why the estimate changed, asks for options, protects the outcome, checks dependencies, and communicates early.
- **Probe:** What if the date is contractually committed?

### 2. Your CEO wants five features, but the team can deliver two. How do you decide?
- **Level / time:** Advanced / 25 min.
- **Tests:** Prioritisation, influence, executive communication.
- **Task:** Create criteria, compare options, and make a recommendation.
- **Strong signals:** Clarifies objective, separates mandatory from optional, and explains opportunity cost.
- **Probe:** What if the CEO disagrees with your recommendation?

### 3. A critical feature launches and adoption is low. What do you do in the first week?
- **Level / time:** Intermediate / 25 min.
- **Tests:** Launch diagnosis, analytics, customer discovery.
- **Task:** Build a one-week investigation plan.
- **Strong signals:** Checks exposure, eligibility, awareness, usability, value, instrumentation, and segment differences.
- **Probe:** What if usage is low but customer outcomes improve?

### 4. Two teams depend on the same platform team and both say their work is urgent. How do you resolve it?
- **Level / time:** Advanced / 30 min.
- **Tests:** Dependency management, negotiation, portfolio judgment.
- **Task:** Establish a decision process and sequence.
- **Strong signals:** Makes impact and cost of delay visible, identifies shared options, and names a decision owner.
- **Probe:** What if both have executive sponsors?

### 5. A large customer requests a custom feature that would delay the roadmap. What do you do?
- **Level / time:** Advanced / 25 min.
- **Tests:** Customer management, product integrity, commercial judgment.
- **Task:** Decide whether to build, configure, partner, or decline.
- **Strong signals:** Quantifies strategic value, reuse, support burden, and precedent risk.
- **Probe:** What if the customer is at risk of churning?

### 6. How would you create a roadmap when the strategy is unclear?
- **Level / time:** Intermediate / 25 min.
- **Tests:** Problem discovery, alignment, planning under uncertainty.
- **Task:** Explain the first steps and a useful interim roadmap.
- **Strong signals:** Surfaces strategic choices, identifies evidence gaps, and separates discovery from commitments.
- **Probe:** How do you protect delivery while strategy is being clarified?

### 7. A launch is ready from Engineering but not from Customer Support. What do you do?
- **Level / time:** Intermediate / 20 min.
- **Tests:** Operational readiness, customer risk, launch judgment.
- **Task:** Decide whether to launch, phase, or delay.
- **Strong signals:** Understands support impact, readiness evidence, customer harm, and rollout controls.
- **Probe:** What if the launch is tied to a public event?

### 8. A team delivers on time but repeatedly misses the intended customer outcome. How would you respond?
- **Level / time:** Advanced / 30 min.
- **Tests:** Outcome orientation, discovery, operating model.
- **Task:** Diagnose the pattern and propose changes.
- **Strong signals:** Examines problem selection, assumptions, instrumentation, quality, and feedback loops rather than blaming execution.
- **Probe:** What would you change in planning and review rituals?

### 9. How would you prioritise reliability work against new features?
- **Level / time:** Advanced / 25 min.
- **Tests:** Technical judgment, risk, customer impact.
- **Task:** Build a decision model with leading indicators.
- **Strong signals:** Considers incident severity, affected users, error budgets or service objectives where appropriate, and the cost of degraded trust.
- **Probe:** What if the reliability risk has not yet caused a major incident?

### 10. A stakeholder bypasses the roadmap and commits your team to a date. What do you do?
- **Level / time:** Advanced / 25 min.
- **Tests:** Influence, accountability, governance.
- **Task:** Resolve the immediate commitment and prevent recurrence.
- **Strong signals:** Addresses customer impact first, clarifies authority, communicates facts, and improves the commitment process.
- **Probe:** What if the stakeholder is your manager?

### 11. A project is halfway complete and new evidence suggests the problem is not important. What do you do?
- **Level / time:** Advanced / 25 min.
- **Tests:** Sunk-cost awareness, evidence-led decisions.
- **Task:** Decide whether to stop, pivot, or continue.
- **Strong signals:** Separates money already spent from future cost, checks evidence quality, and identifies reversible next steps.
- **Probe:** How would you communicate stopping the work?

### 12. You inherit a roadmap with 40 committed items and no clear outcomes. Where do you start?
- **Level / time:** Senior / 30 min.
- **Tests:** Portfolio triage, leadership, alignment.
- **Task:** Create a 30-day assessment and reset process.
- **Strong signals:** Maps items to outcomes, commitments, dependencies, capacity, and confidence; avoids cancelling blindly.
- **Probe:** What would you preserve during the transition?

## Post-answer review

Record your chosen decision, the most important assumption, the trade-off you made, the person who must be aligned, and the next checkpoint. If any of these is missing, repeat the answer.
