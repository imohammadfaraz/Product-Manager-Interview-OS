# Case Study: Streaming subscription churn

**Category:** Consumer Subscription  
**Suggested time:** 30-35 minutes  
**Difficulty:** Intermediate to Advanced  
**Scenario status:** Fictional practice case. All organisations, figures, and situations are invented for learning.

## Candidate brief

A fictional streaming service sees monthly subscriber churn rise after a price increase. Leadership wants to know whether to reverse the increase, change packaging, or improve the product.

## Information supplied

Fictional figures: monthly churn rose from 4.0% to 5.1% after a price change. Average revenue per paying account rose 8%. Cancellation surveys mention price, lack of relevant new content, and shared-account changes. Churn is higher among customers on the lowest-priced plan, but that plan also has a different acquisition mix.

## Your assignment

Recommend an investigation and a response that considers revenue, retention, and customer value.

## Work area

Before reading the facilitator notes, write your response using these headings:

1. Objective and assumptions.
2. User / stakeholder segments.
3. Problem statement.
4. Evidence to inspect and questions to ask.
5. Options considered.
6. Recommendation and first release.
7. Metrics and guardrails.
8. Risks, dependencies, and decision gates.

## Candidate prompts and hints

Compare affected cohorts with suitable unaffected groups if available. Separate voluntary and involuntary churn, plan, tenure, acquisition source, usage, and content preferences. Review price sensitivity and customer lifetime value without assuming all churn is bad.

## Facilitator notes: one defensible approach

Do not immediately reverse the price increase. Estimate net revenue retention or contribution impact by segment, test packaging or annual-plan options, and investigate content engagement. Consider targeted retention offers only when economics and fairness support them.

## Suggested success measures

Primary: contribution or recurring revenue retained by cohort. Guardrails: churn, downgrade rate, viewing satisfaction, refund and complaint rate, and acquisition conversion.

## Follow-up probes

What if the higher price increases revenue despite churn? What if the content gap is seasonal? Which customers should receive an offer?
